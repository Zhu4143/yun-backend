# Yun particle avatar POC

## Scope

Replace the current fade plus portrait-overlay experiment with one particle field in `ParticleVinylBackground`. The existing record point set remains the only portrait renderer; its shader moves those points from the record into the anime portrait and back, alongside the existing liquid-metal background. The three supplied clips provide wake, listening, and speaking motion. Their pale, stable background makes a local color key a suitable segmentation path; the installed MediaPipe package has no task model asset, so this POC does not add a model download or runtime inference dependency.

## Data and motion path

- Copy the three original clips into `public/avatar-motion/` with explicit wake/listening/speaking names. Keep them muted and invisible; only their masked pixels become particle coordinates and colors.
- Build a deterministic foreground mask from the video border color, smooth its edge, and use a nearest-foreground field to keep each particle attached to nearby subject pixels as frames advance. Use a tight background tolerance with four-neighbor border connectivity; do not fill a geometric torso box, since the pale studio backdrop can then be mistaken for sweater pixels. Keep particle indices stable across every clip.
- Preserve the supplied video's 4:3 aspect ratio and fit the portrait within 94% of viewport height and 98% of viewport width, anchoring its lower edge to the screen bottom as requested; recompute the fit on resize. Hold the human camera fixed and ignore background drag/wheel input while a portrait is present.
- Sample portrait motion at 160x120 / 256x192 / 320x240 for low / medium / high quality. Keep the existing particle budgets; size portrait sprites from projected source-pixel spacing and canvas DPR, with a fuller portrait alpha profile to keep the image clear as the window grows.
- Drive shader progress from the animation frame ref instead of throttled React state, and use a 3.4-second handoff with quintic easing for a slower, continuous formation and return.
- Calibrate each clip once from its initial foreground head bounds so wake, listening, and speaking share the same head scale, center, and top. Keep that transform fixed through the clip; interpolate particle positions, UVs, and colors between published frames and soften clip changes and seeks.
- Use one-shot playback for wake, looping playback for listening, and automatically detected mouth-motion intervals for speaking. Bridge the closed-mouth gaps with varied wake/listening gestures, bounded by the available gap, instead of hard-seeking through the speaking clip. Use TTS output bands as read-only motion energy while Yun speaks.
- Interpolate the particle positions, breathing, breakup, and voice response in the existing vertex shader. Do not add a portrait canvas or video overlay.

## State and integration

Keep wake/capture/chat/TTS lifecycle ownership in the existing app and voice hooks. Derive a read-only portrait phase from those signals and pass through vinyl idle, breaking, human forming, one-shot wake motion, listening, thinking, speaking, human dispersing, and vinyl reforming. Formation holds the wake clip on its first frame; the wake clip then plays once before semantic listening/speaking motion starts. Attach the wake completion handler even when formation reuses the already-mounted video, so the render phase can advance into listening. Returning phases pause the hidden clip while the shader morphs back. A development-only debug control can preview each phase without microphone access.
